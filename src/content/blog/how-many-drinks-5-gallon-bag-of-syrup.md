A 5 gallon bag-in-box of soda syrup makes **30 gallons of finished soda** at the standard 5 to 1 mix. That's **3,840 ounces**, or about **342 sixteen-ounce drinks** with a normal 30% ice fill, and **240** if you pour to the rim with no ice.

The number most people quote is the no-ice number. It's wrong for every restaurant that puts ice in the cup, which is all of them.

My favorite part of every bag-in-box is the half-perforated triangle that says "press with thumb." It expects you to punch through two solid layers of cardboard with the power of one thumb. Once you get it open, here is what is actually inside.

## The short answer, by box size

Every number below assumes a 5 to 1 mix (5 parts water, 1 part syrup), which is what most colas and most fountain syrups run.

| Box size | Syrup | Finished soda | 16 oz drinks with 30% ice | 16 oz drinks, no ice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 gallon | 640 oz | 30 gallons (3,840 oz) | 342 | 240 |
| 3 gallon | 384 oz | 18 gallons (2,304 oz) | 205 | 144 |
| 2.5 gallon | 320 oz | 15 gallons (1,920 oz) | 171 | 120 |

The 3 gallon math matches what WebstaurantStore lists on its 3 gallon Mountain Dew box: about 18 gallons of prepared soda, or 192 twelve-ounce servings with no ice ([WebstaurantStore](https://www.webstaurantstore.com/mountain-dew-bag-in-box-beverage-soda-syrup-3-gallon/103BIBPMTDEW.html)).

## How the math works

Three steps. You can do it on a bar napkin.

1. **Syrup in ounces.** A gallon is 128 ounces. 5 gallons is 640 oz, 3 gallons is 384 oz, 2.5 gallons is 320 oz.
2. **Times the mix.** At 5 to 1, every ounce of syrup becomes 6 ounces of soda (5 water plus 1 syrup). 640 x 6 = 3,840 oz.
3. **Divided by the soda in one cup.** Not the cup size. The soda that actually goes in it after ice. A 16 oz cup with 30% ice holds 11.2 oz of soda. 3,840 / 11.2 = 342 drinks.

That third step is where people get it wrong. Ice takes up room, and room you fill with ice is room you don't fill with syrup.

## What changes the count

**Ice fill.** 30% is a normal dine-in fill. Drive-thru cups often run 40% or more. More ice means more drinks per box and a lower cost per drink. A 16 oz cup at 40% ice gets you 400 drinks from a 5 gallon box instead of 342.

**Mix ratio.** Diet sodas often run 4 to 1, and some specialty syrups run 6 to 1. At 4 to 1, a 5 gallon box makes 3,200 oz of soda, or about 285 sixteen-ounce drinks with ice. If your diet box runs out faster than your regular, that's usually why.

**Refills.** Refills don't change how many drinks a box makes. They change how many customers a box serves. At the common rule of thumb of 1.4 drinks per customer with free refills, 342 drinks covers about 244 customers.

**A dispenser that's out of calibration.** If the valve is pouring 4 to 1 when it should pour 5 to 1, you're using 20% more syrup on every drink. The box runs out early, and the soda tastes heavy. The distributor will calibrate it for free if you ask.

## Why the count matters

The drink count is how you get your cost per drink. Take what you paid for the box and divide by the drinks it makes.

A $90 box of branded cola that makes 342 drinks is about **$0.26 of syrup per drink**. Add the cup, lid, straw and a cent of CO2 and you're near **$0.45**. On a $3.49 drink that's a pour cost around 13% before refills.

That's the cheap part of the cup. For the full breakdown, including what free refills really cost you, read [how much a free refill actually costs](/blog/soda-fountain-margin-free-refills/).

## Use the count to check your inventory

If you know how many drinks a box should make, you can tell when it doesn't.

Track boxes used against fountain drinks sold for a week. If a 5 gallon box should cover about 342 sixteen-ounce drinks and you're only getting 250, something is off. Usually it's one of these, in this order:

1. The mix ratio drifted.
2. The cup vendor changed the cup.
3. Somebody is pouring with no ice.
4. A leak in a syrup line.
5. Drinks going out that never hit the register.

## Common questions

**How many gallons of soda does a 5 gallon bag-in-box make?**
30 gallons at a 5 to 1 mix. 640 oz of syrup becomes 3,840 oz of soda.

**How much soda does 2.5 gallons of syrup make?**
15 gallons, or 1,920 oz. That's about 171 sixteen-ounce drinks with 30% ice.

**How many 12 oz drinks are in a 5 gallon box?**
About 457 with 30% ice and 320 with no ice.

**Does a 3 gallon box make 3/5 of a 5 gallon box?**
Yes. 384 oz of syrup versus 640 oz, so about 60% of the drinks.

**What's the cost per drink from a bag-in-box?**
Box price divided by drinks per box, plus the cup, lid, straw and CO2. Plug yours into the [fountain drink cost calculator](/calculators/fountain-cost/).
